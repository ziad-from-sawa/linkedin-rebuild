# Anti-AI writing rules

Sawa's house rules. Every email and post this workflow writes must pass them.

These rules apply to writing produced for every company, client, prospect, and test-client entity. They are universal. They do not describe any entity's voice.

### Hard rules

1. **Never use em dashes or en dashes.** Use a period, comma, colon, parentheses, or rewrite the sentence.
2. **Do not use the "it is not X, it is Y" construction or close variants.** State the point directly.
3. **Do not use revelation hooks.** Remove openings such as "here is what nobody tells you" and say the observation.
4. **Do not use fragmentary rhetorical setups.** Avoid patterns such as "The best part?" followed by an answer.
5. **Do not use philosophical flips or polished reframes.** Write one coherent thought instead of manufacturing a paradox.
6. **Remove corporate and marketing residue.** Avoid hype terms such as leverage, utilize, streamline, seamless, robust, scalable, holistic, ecosystem, deep dive, synergy, cutting-edge, game-changer, pain point, thought leadership, and similar language. Use plain words that name the action.
7. **Do not use formal essay summaries.** Avoid "in conclusion," "to summarize," "it is worth noting," and similar scaffolding.
8. **Do not add AI preambles.** Start with the requested work, not an announcement about the draft.
9. **Do not stack negation fragments.** Avoid sequences such as "No hype. No jargon. Just results."
10. **Do not split one continuous thought into artificial sentence fragments.** Keep sentence-initial conjunctions only when they introduce a genuine new beat.
11. **Do not stack multiple "no X" phrases inside one sentence.** State the positive claim or keep only the strongest contrast.
12. **Do not use a colon to manufacture drama between two complete sentences.** Colons remain valid for lists, labels, dialogue, times, URLs, and technical formats.
13. **Test filler intensifiers.** Remove actually, really, genuinely, literally, truly, and basically when the sentence means the same without them.
14. **Do not use "real" as a vague authenticity signal.** Name the concrete category. Factual uses, such as "real numbers," are acceptable.
15. **Limit vague nouns and hand-waves.** Replace stuff, things, and whatever with a concrete noun whenever possible.
16. **Remove "whole" when it adds only emphasis.** Keep it only when it changes the factual meaning.
17. **Limit three-beat cadence.** Use at most one deliberate triad in a piece. Rewrite repeated three-item rhythms.
18. **Prefer plain linking verbs.** Avoid inflated substitutes such as serves as, stands as, functions as, boasts, and comes packed with.
19. **Do not use vague attribution.** Name the source behind a claim. Cut the claim when no source exists.
20. **Do not announce significance.** Replace phrases such as "a pivotal moment" or "a defining shift" with the fact that made the event matter.
21. **Remove stock phrases.** Avoid "here is the thing," "let that sink in," "make no mistake," and similar templates.
22. **Mix sentence lengths.** Do not build drama through three or more consecutive short sentences or fragments.
23. **Do not proclaim emotion as a shortcut.** Show the concrete moment or state the emotion plainly inside a sentence.
24. **Avoid decorative metaphors and clever stand-ins.** Use a metaphor only when it explains the idea more clearly than literal language.
25. **Say the point once.** Remove throat-clearing, announcements of the point, and immediate re-explanations.
26. **Do not hide the point in a trailing "because" clause.** Lead with the specific content.
27. **Do not use additive negative parallelism.** Avoid "not only X, but also Y" and "does not just X, it Y." Make the stronger claim directly.
28. **Remove trailing participle padding.** Cut comma-plus-ing endings that merely editorialize about meaning or importance.
29. **Do not cycle through synonyms to avoid repetition.** Repeat the plain noun or use a pronoun.
30. **Do not close with unsupported future speculation.** End on a concrete fact, image, decision, or plain sign-off.

### Final audit

Before approving a draft:

- Search for forbidden dash characters.
- Check every contrast construction.
- Replace vague, inflated, or unsupported claims.
- Confirm that each factual claim has a source.
- Read the draft aloud and remove filler that does no work.
- Confirm that the ending stops on something concrete.
