LinkedIn Rebuild

Ziad Hassan · founder, PM Charlotte Driscoll Yuri Mukai Dan Chauvin

1. Startup in one line

What you're building and for whom

Sawa is an AI-native marketing agency, starting with a narrow focus on B2B content, for founders and CEOs of B2B companies with 10 to 200 staff.

2. The workflow

Its name, and why it matters most to your startup right now

LinkedIn Rebuild. One founder's LinkedIn URL goes in. A teardown of their profile, a rebuilt version of it, a first post and two emails for Ziad come out.

  • Biggest demand. LinkedIn content is what clients ask us for most. It feeds the three plans we run for each client: Grow (LinkedIn content fills the top of the funnel), Engage (a newsletter turns readers into subscribers) and Convert (emails get them to an event, a discount or an upsell).
  • Revenue. Each client pays a setup fee, then a monthly retainer to keep it running. Winning founders is the job right now.
  • The pitch. Ziad wants every first email to show the founder their own profile, rebuilt, so no two pitches look the same. This workflow makes one from a single URL.

3. User and trigger

Who runs it, and what kicks it off

User: Ziad. He feeds in the URL and uses everything that comes out.

Trigger: Ziad finds a founder he wants as a client and runs /rebuild <their LinkedIn URL> in Claude Code.

4. Input

Exactly what goes in, with a real example

One public LinkedIn profile URL of a B2B founder or CEO. Nothing else.

Real example: https://www.linkedin.com/in/ziad-has/. Ziad's own profile. Sawa is a small B2B company, so it fits the target, and it is our model of a good profile.

6. Output

Exactly what comes out, with an example of what "great" looks like and how you'll judge it
What comes out

One folder per founder, out/<name>/:

  • teardown.md
  • mockup.html
  • first-post.md
  • email-1.md
  • email-2-bump.md
Great profile: Ziad's 7 checks
  1. Headshot on a solid color background
  2. Website link under the name
  3. A banner
  4. About section filled out, in bullets
  5. At least one featured post with an image
  6. Short, clear headline
  7. Follow button instead of Connect
What great looks like
  • Mockup: passes all 7 checks. Ziad's profile is the example.
  • Teardown: every check carries the fact behind it. Example line, format only:

3 · Banner · Fix · background_picture_url is empty. Add a banner that says what the company does, in its own colors.

  • Email 1: opens with a detail only this founder would recognize, names 2 fixes from the teardown, offers the mockup and ends on a yes or no question. Under 100 words. No links.
  • Email 2: two or three lines, a few days later, with calendly.com/ziad-startsawa/15min for a 15-minute call.
  • First post: in their voice, on a topic they already post about. Every fact comes from their profile or posts.

We have no past email that won. The team drafts version 1 on Ziad's URL, Ziad marks it up, and the marked-up draft becomes the bar.

How we judge it
  • Every number matches the pulled JSON.
  • The email names one specific thing from their profile or posts.
  • Email 1 has no link.
  • Zero em dashes. Passes the anti-AI rules.
  • Ziad would send it without edits.
  • 3 runs on 3 founders give the same quality.

7. Human checkpoints

Where a person reviews before anything is sent, posted, or decided
  • Before outreach: Ziad reads the whole folder. Any number that doesn't match the data stops the run.
  • Before sending: emails land as drafts in Ziad's own inbox. He edits and sends them himself.
  • Before the bump: only if there is no reply, and only when Ziad says so.
  • Posting: the first post is a sample for the pitch. Nothing posts to anyone's account.

8. Definition of done

The smallest genuinely useful version, achievable in about 80 minutes
  • /rebuild <url> in Claude Code writes all five files for one founder.
  • It runs on 3 founder URLs with the same quality each time.
  • Plain files are enough. The mockup is HTML, and Ziad pastes the emails into his inbox.

9. Stretch goals

What you'd add with more time
  • UI/UX wrapper: a local page with a URL box and a Generate button. It runs claude -p "/rebuild <url>" and shows the five outputs, with the mockup rendered.
  • The banner exported as a PNG at 1584 x 396, ready to upload.
  • A new headshot on a solid color background.
  • Emails created straight in Ziad's Gmail drafts.
  • Batch mode: a list of URLs in, one folder each.

10. Workstreams

How three teammates can split the build (core workflow; inputs, data, and connections; testing and demo)

Inputs, data, and connections

Teammate 1 · step 1
Pull the data
  • Make a free Apify account. Put your own token in .env as APIFY_API_TOKEN.
  • Profile: apimaestro/linkedin-profile-batch-scraper-no-cookies-required
  • Posts: apimaestro/linkedin-batch-profile-posts-scraper. It has no date filter, so keep the last 90 days yourself.
  • Skip harvestapi actors. Free plans cap them at 10 runs.
  • Save JSON to out/<name>/raw/. Ziad's URL first, then the 3 test URLs.
Done when 4 profiles are pulled and each has headline, about, banner, website, featured and follower fields.

Core workflow

Teammate 2 · steps 2 to 5
Produce the teardown and mockup
  • Score against profile-checklist.md, write teardown.md.
  • Build mockup.html in their brand colors. Open it in a browser and compare it with Ziad's profile.
Produce the first post
  • Read their posts and About first. Write one post on a topic they already cover. Invent nothing.
Produce the email
  • Email 1 and the bump, in Ziad's voice. The team writes the copy.
  • Save steps 2 to 5 as .claude/commands/rebuild.md.
Done when /rebuild on Ziad's URL writes all five files.

Testing and demo

Teammate 3 · checks and video
Test
  • Run /rebuild on the 3 test URLs.
  • Check every output against the judging list in section 6.
  • Search every file for em and en dashes.
  • Log what breaks. Hand fixes to the core builder one at a time.
Demo
  • Record the 2-minute video: real URL in, real folder out (due 9 pm Tuesday).
Done when 3 runs give 3 complete folders of the same quality.

Ziad owns the problem and judges the output without taking the keyboard. Everyone drives for at least 10 minutes, and the teammate newest to AI goes first. Whoever finishes first takes the UI/UX wrapper.
